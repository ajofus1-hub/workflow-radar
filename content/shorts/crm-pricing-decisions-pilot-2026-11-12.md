# Workflow Radar short-form pilot: CRM pricing decisions

**Prepared:** 12 November 2026  
**Status:** Draft only — not published or scheduled  
**Purpose:** Test whether concise, useful education can bring qualified small-business visitors to Workflow Radar without relying on affiliate claims.

## Distribution and monetisation decision

Metricool currently has one connected YouTube brand for this account. Its available analytics returned 0 subscribers on 9–11 November 2026 and no reported video views or revenue in the available rows through 12 November. The connector only returned data from 9 November onward, so this is not a historical channel audit.

YouTube is available to eligible creators in India. Current official entry points:
- Expanded YPP: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days. This unlocks eligible fan-funding and shopping features, not full watch-page ad revenue.
- Full ad revenue: 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days.
- YouTube has announced higher entry thresholds for new applicants from 1 February 2027: 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 valid public watch hours in 365 days or 20 million valid Shorts views in 90 days.
- India YouTube payments are available by wire transfer. Payment thresholds depend on the payments-account currency; the USD account threshold is $100.

**Decision:** Treat Shorts as a low-cost discovery experiment, not a near-term revenue forecast. The connected brand has no measurable audience signal yet. Do not spend on ads, production subscriptions, or paid promotion. Do not publish repetitive templated videos; each video must contain distinct original explanation and useful examples. No revenue is assumed.

## Short 1 — Flat-rate is not automatically cheaper

**Length:** 25–35 seconds  
**Format:** Worked example with handwritten-style numbers  
**Audience question:** “At what team size does flat-rate CRM pricing make sense?”

**Voiceover:**

“Is a flat-rate CRM always cheaper? Not necessarily. Here's a simple example. Imagine a CRM charges 900 rupees per user each month, while another plan costs 4,000 rupees flat. With four users, the per-user plan costs 3,600. With five users, it costs 4,500. So the flat plan becomes cheaper at five users — before add-ons, taxes and feature differences. Use your actual prices, not the headline price.”

**On-screen sequence:**
1. “₹900 × 4 users = ₹3,600”
2. “₹900 × 5 users = ₹4,500”
3. “Flat plan = ₹4,000”
4. “Break-even: 5 users (example only)”
5. “Add-ons and features can change the answer”

**Visual direction:** Show the multiplication and comparison clearly. Keep all numbers labelled “hypothetical example”. No vendor logos or stock claims.

**CTA:** “Save this example before comparing your next CRM renewal.”

## Short 2 — The four costs missing from a CRM headline price

**Length:** 30–40 seconds  
**Format:** Fast checklist, with one concrete explanation per item

**Voiceover:**

“Before choosing a CRM, don't stop at the monthly seat price. Check four things. First, paid add-ons for calling, WhatsApp, storage or automation. Second, minimum seat requirements. Third, annual prepayment and setup fees. Fourth, export limits and the features included in the plan. A low starting price can still be the wrong fit if your team needs a feature that costs extra. Compare the actual invoice, not just the pricing banner.”

**On-screen sequence:**
- “1. Add-ons and usage”
- “2. Minimum seats”
- “3. Annual commitment / setup”
- “4. Feature and export limits”
- End card: “Compare total cost + fit”

**Visual direction:** Four distinct checklist cards; show a sample invoice with fictional/blank line items, not a real vendor invoice.

**CTA:** “Keep this checklist for your next software trial.”

## Short 3 — A five-task CRM trial that prevents a bad purchase

**Length:** 35–45 seconds  
**Format:** Practical mini-challenge

**Voiceover:**

“Don't choose a CRM after watching only a feature demo. Test the same five tasks in every product. Add a lead from your phone. Assign it to a teammate. Set a follow-up reminder. Move it through your sales stages. Then export the record. Time each task and note anything that requires a paid upgrade. This tells you more about daily usability than a long feature list. Try the same test with your real workflow before paying.”

**On-screen sequence:**
1. Add a lead on mobile
2. Assign it
3. Set a reminder
4. Move the sales stage
5. Export the record
6. “Same five tasks. Every trial.”

**Visual direction:** Screen-record a locally created demo dataset with fictional names and phone numbers. Do not expose real customer information.

**CTA:** “Use the five-task test before committing to an annual plan.”

## Production guardrails

- Use original narration, original examples, and a distinct visual structure for each video.
- Do not reuse third-party clips, music, logos, screenshots, or competitor footage without permission.
- Do not claim that any CRM is “best”, cheapest, or endorsed by Workflow Radar.
- Keep example prices clearly hypothetical.
- Do not add affiliate links to these scripts. If a later version includes an affiliate CTA, disclose the relationship plainly and recommend the lowest suitable plan.
- Avoid repetitive, mass-produced templates; the substance and examples must differ materially.
- Do not use fake urgency, fabricated results, engagement bait, or misleading thumbnails.
- Do not publish or schedule automatically from this draft. Production and account-level publication remain separate decisions.

## Measurement plan for a future pilot

Record separately:
- Shorts published
- Reach and engaged views
- Average percentage viewed / retention
- Channel subscribers gained
- Profile or description link clicks (if available)
- Qualified visits to the relevant Workflow Radar tool
- Tool interactions (counts only; never record user-entered prices)
- Outbound affiliate clicks
- Signups and paid referrals
- Earned commission, cleared commission, and cash received

Do not infer site visits or sales from views. Do not attribute a sale without an auditable referral record.

## Go / no-go

**GO:** keep this three-video pilot as a free, organic discovery experiment only if the owner wants to use the connected Workflow Radar channel and can review the final videos.

**NO-GO:** do not build a separate paid storefront, buy production tools, run ads, or forecast YouTube revenue from the current zero-signal analytics. Reassess after the channel has enough published content and a measurable audience.
